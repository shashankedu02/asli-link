Is this link really your bank?

Paste a link or the whole SMS or WhatsApp message. Asli Link checks whether the address belongs to the bank or government office it claims to be, using India's official namespaces: .bank.in, .gov.in and .nic.in.

Nothing you paste is stored, and no link is opened.

How it decides

  1. Find the real owner of the address. The part that decides who owns a website is the end of the host name, read with the Public Suffix List. In sbi.bank.in.secure-login.xyz the owner is secure-login.xyz, not SBI.
  2. Check official namespaces. Only RBI-regulated banks can register under .bank.in (RBI circular, April 2025). .gov.in and .nic.in are for government. A short list of older bank domains and institutions (such as RBI and NPCI) is curated by hand.
  3. Look for impersonation. Bank or department names inside someone else's domain, lookalike spellings (onlinesbl), mixed alphabets (a Cyrillic а in аxisbank), fake namespaces (bank-in.com), and messages that name a bank but link elsewhere.
  4. Say only what can be established. If none of that applies, the answer is Not recognised, which is not the same as safe.

What this cannot tell you

  • An official domain says who owns the site, not that a request is genuine. Banks never ask for your OTP, PIN or password by link.
  • Official sites can be compromised. A shortened link hides its destination, so it is never opened.
  • The curated list does not include every bank, department or company. Missing from the list means not recognised, never fake.
  • If you have lost money, call 1930 or report at cybercrime.gov.in immediately. To report a fraud message, use Sanchar Saathi (Chakshu).
